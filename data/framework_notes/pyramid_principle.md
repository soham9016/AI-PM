# The Pyramid Principle

Developed by Barbara Minto, the Pyramid Principle structures written
communication so the reader gets the answer first, then the supporting
logic, then the detail — the opposite order from how the analysis was
actually done.

Structure:
1. **Governing thought** — the single main answer/recommendation, stated
   in one or two sentences.
2. **Key line** — 3-5 supporting arguments, each MECE relative to the
   others, that together prove the governing thought.
3. **Supporting evidence** — data, findings, and analysis beneath each
   key-line argument.

Grouping rule: ideas at any level of the pyramid must be logically the
same *kind* of idea (all reasons, all steps, all findings) and should be
summarizable by the idea directly above them (SCQA: Situation,
Complication, Question, Answer, is a common way to introduce the top of
the pyramid before the governing thought).

For this system, the synthesizer agent's job is to take verdicts on each
hypothesis and re-order them into a pyramid: lead with the overall
answer/recommendation, then group hypothesis verdicts into 3-5 key-line
arguments, then attach evidence under each.
